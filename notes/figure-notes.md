# Figure notes

All four figures are editable TikZ in the paper's notation; they await the authors' approval.

1. fig-dependencies (Section 3, Figure 1). Idea: the order of the steps — which statements feed
   which, and where the contradiction and unimodularity enter. Question answered: "how do the four
   steps combine?" Exact (a dependency graph of labelled statements); no quantities drawn.
   Revised after review C12/R8: Part I and Part II columns contain only the four steps and their
   ingredients; the sprinkling bound sits in the left column and feeds both the assembly
   (Theorem 3.1) and the reduction (Theorem 2.1); the former "Step 5" box is "Assembly"; formulas are
   kept on one line (\mbox) and boxes are not hyphenated.
2. fig-corridor (Section 3.4, Figure 2, placed after Definition 3.9). Idea: on Cor_w the corridor is
   the only open link between the exterior clusters of u and v; all its edges are pivotal for x<->y
   iff x,y lie in these two clusters (either order) and the clusters are distinct (Lemma 9.2).
   Schematic.
3. fig-chain (Section 6.2, Figure 3). Idea: the switched root cluster is a chain A - e - B - e - B ...
   of pieces attached by single open seams; cutting the seams recovers all pieces (basis of the
   inverse code, Lemma 6.5). Schematic; pieces B_{i_j}, seams e_{i_j}.
4. fig-escapes (Section 10, Figure 4). Idea: the raw path starts and ends with halves of geodesic
   segments; the escapes leave u and v along the opposite halves and continue as independent walks,
   so they are unlikely to meet the raw path, and their endpoints see a smoothed connection kernel.
   Schematic. Notation updated: middle walk pi (formerly eta), tails zeta, zeta'.
